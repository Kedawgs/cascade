# Cascade v3.2: the Task 42 campaign (2026-11-12 to 2026-11-14)

The record of plan Task 42's runs on the Cascade v3.2 contracts (`contracts/`, branch `v3.2`), written for an
auditor: the invariant campaigns, the 3,000 × 64 long run and its rerun, the four fix rounds and their reviews, the
one contract change made during the campaign (Revision 27), the residuals the campaign found or confirmed, the
Halmos attempts, the 32-seed sweep, static analysis and size gate D. Commits are on `v3.2`. The design is
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-cascade-v3.2-design.md` (the spec); `contracts/README.md` carries the tables
cited here ("Exit liveness", "Vault favour", "Static analysis").

## Summary

- **No defect was found in `contracts/src` by the campaigns.** Every invariant failure was a gap in a test's model
  or bound: three in the long run, two in its rerun, and one in each sweep (seed 112, an exits judge; seed 115, the
  copied-key walk's chained relay outages). Each was diagnosed by replaying its saved counterexample, fixed in the
  test, and pinned as a regression test.
- **The campaigns found three properties of the design.** The orphan at a supply of 0 (the long run) and a leaver's
  forfeit vesting to whoever holds (the rerun) are stated residuals. An adoption restarting the reward schedule
  (fix round 4) was fixed before launch by Revision 27, the adoption drip.
- **The contracts changed once during the campaign:** Revision 27 (6d3eb21 and its review fixes). Every other Task
  42 commit changed tests, scripts or documents. **Revision 28** (Base's 0.2-second blocks, section 1) changed the
  source after it, so the final sweep ran on that source, 7f3dcee.
- **The final 32-seed sweep (section 8):** 31 of 32 seeds passed every test; seed 115's one failure was the test's
  model, fixed in b88b44e. With the fix the copied-key campaign passes at all 32 seeds.
- **Static analysis:** Slither 0.11.5, 171 results; Aderyn 0.6.8, 5 High and 21 Low issue kinds. Every finding has
  a verdict in `contracts/README.md`; none needs a code change. Both re-run on the final source after Revision 28:
  the same results, apart from Revision 28's renames and two new underscored constants (section 9).
- **Size gate D passed:** `CascadeVault` 22,396 B, 680 B under the gate's 23,076 B; after Revision 28, 22,655 B, 421
  B under it.
- **Halmos:** one of three properties proven; the other two did not finish under yices or Bitwuzla, and fuzzing
  covers them (section 7).
- **The final head, b88b44e** (source 7f3dcee): the forced suite 109 suites, 1,440 passed, 0 failed, 27 skipped
  (the fork tests); the build checks 7 of 7; the build scripts' tests 140 of 140; `forge fmt --check` clean;
  `forge lint` exit 0 with no errors.

What Task 42 added:

- **I-30 and I-31** (core; dbab5d7, a607dc1). I-30: each delivery is checked against its three floors apart (the
  user's, the keeper's, and the oracle's 30-minute value less the slippage allowance). I-31: a `stake` action
  through the staker against the 95% buy floor (`NoOracle`, the oracle's "OLD" revert and `BelowOracleFloor`
  predicted), and no delivery while a pool's mean liquidity is under its floor (a pool-thinning switch; a
  `deliverThin` draw was added when the "no depth check" mutation survived without it).
- **I-37, every claim has a non-owner exit** (`Exits.invariants.t.sol`, `ExitHandler`; e578300, then every round).
  From each state the campaign reaches, with one more blocker applied (14 scenarios), the users, anyone and the
  keeper's routine alone (about 19 days: four rounds of exits a day apart, the payout delay, the park timeout) must
  get each user's money out of seven kinds of holding: a deposit in the open batch, a token park, a native park,
  shares, a queued payout, a claimable payout and a message in flight. The exceptions are what an explicit
  owner-only table's closers hold (the vault paused, the launch door removed, a primary disabled, stake truly lost,
  a queued payout behind a rail closed for good, ForeverMoney's migration, the chain's minimum, the founder's
  cushion, and I-28's payout through a vault primary the door never activated). It is judged both ways: money left
  with a path open fails, and money the table says is held that got out fails (the over-claim check). Since fix
  round 1 each user's wallet must also rise by what left its holdings, less a stated rounding bound (the receipt
  check). A second contract runs at the launch limits (a 10 TAO entry limit, a 10 TAO daily floor, a 15 TAO
  message cap).
- **I-38, no one gains except by time held** (`Favor.invariants.t.sol`, `FavorHandler`; b817649, then every round),
  at the launch's 30 days of reward vesting and 10% cut. `noActorGainsByActing`: across every action the acting
  actor's wealth does not rise, and across every action the attacker does not make, its wealth does not rise beyond
  rounding. `gainsOnlyByTimeShare`: a holder who may leave gains at most its time share of the rewards that
  accrued while it held (net of the cut), of gifts as they vest, of donations as they land, and of the vest above
  accrual that departed holders' stake funded. `gainsAreConserved`: every gain is matched by a loss or a reward.
  The campaign covers the owner's cancel and a refused delivery's retry; a park, a bounce and stake missing are held
  by the pins and campaigns the README's "Vault favour" names.
- **The forged lane** (adapter; 7fae2d8). `invariant_aForgedMessageMovesNothingItDidNotCarry`, with dedicated draws
  for F-2 (a forged redeem-only batch from the door), for the sweep a forged call sets off, and for forged copies of
  real messages.
- **B9, minimum hits** (`MinimumHits.sol`). Each file names its rare states. Each run's counters are summed across
  the file's runs and campaigns and asserted against their minimums only when `CASCADE_MIN_HITS_RUNS` is set (the
  long run sets 3,000). A minimum is a quarter of what the slowest of three 128 × 64 seeds projects for 3,000 runs,
  and 0 (logged, not asserted) when that projection is under 8. Targeted draws bring thin states into reach
  (`moveIntoEmpty`, `toTheCorner`, `depositAtALimit`, `removeTwice`, `partialThenCancel`, `deliverThin`; then
  `crowdTheAllowance`, `reenterThroughTheRouter`, `toTheWalkCost` and `toTheCancelAfterPartial` in rounds 3 and 4).
  The long run is also gated on every tag's runs counted reaching 3,000 and on no invariant reporting a nonzero
  revert count.

**Result: 15 suites, 284 passed, 3 failed.** Every B9 gate was met (every tag's runs counted at least 3,000: the
adapter campaign's 46,353 and the payout campaign's 17,845, for example; no minimum short), and no invariant
reported a nonzero revert count. Every other suite passed, I-38's favour campaign among them. The three failures,
each replayed from its saved counterexample on 8d05725, with its fix proven on a scratch copy first:

1. **`AdapterInvariantTest`, `invariant_aForgedMessageMovesNothingItDidNotCarry`** ("forgedToBase: 1 != 0"; run
   1,484; shrunk to 9 calls: deposit, closeBatch, pauseVault, raiseFloor, pauseVault, strangerSend,
   relayToBittensor, refundParked, forgedToBase). The forged copy of an honest refund reached the stranger's
   recipient actor on Base while that actor was still in its re-entry mode from an earlier honest relay. From
   inside the Base delivery it called the mock router's `deliver` for a door batch already delivered (38.311 TAO);
   the mock minted it again, the vault refused it (`DuplicateBatch`), the adapter parked it, and the invariant saw
   a new park larger than the 3.02 TAO the forged message carried. A model gap: the honest relay turns the mode off,
   the forged-to-Base draw did not, and real CCIP never executes a message twice. Nothing of Cascade's moved.
   **Fix** (c870447): the mode off in `_forgedToBase`; the pin `test_aForgedCopyToBaseCannotReachTheBittensorSide`;
   an opt-in exactly-once mode in `MockRouter`. In round 3 (b5825b9) the pin became four variants showing that each
   guard alone suffices and that with both off the leak appears.
2. **`ExitsAtLaunchLimitsInvariantTest`, `invariant_everyClaimHasANonOwnerExit`** ("money left a user but did not
   reach it: (1, 7, 11,720,617,764, 11,720,597,492)": 20,272 rao short, against a receipt slack of 64 + 4 × 1,000
   rao; run 2,745; shrunk to 9 calls).
3. **`ExitsInvariantTest`, the same invariant** ("(1, 7, 19,806, 0)", twice; shrunk to 7 calls: taoDeposit,
   stakeIdle, taoDeposit, swapAway, doorRound, taoExit, probeExits).

   Failures 2 and 3 share a cause. The last holder exited directly with `acceptMissing` while stake was missing (a
   hotkey swap had moved it), so it was paid at the floor price, and the supply reached 0 with 42.68 TAO of stake
   missing (49 TAO in the launch replay). The routine's relist brought that stake back, held at a supply of 0 by
   the virtual offset's 1e6 shares, so one share was worth about 42,672 rao. Later mints floor to whole shares
   (`CascadeBatchLib.sol:107`), and each lost up to one share's value to the vault (14,788 rao for one user, 19,806
   rao for the other). That is rounding in the vault's favour; the test's receipt slack had assumed under 1 rao an
   operation. **Fix** (72b4eaf): a per-mint slack of one share's value at the delivery's price (tightened in round 3
   to `min(share, ceil(a / (minted + 1)))`, shown to be an upper bound), a counter of coarse mints and two pins
   (`test_aMintOverAnOrphanAtASupplyOf0RoundsAtOneSharesValue`,
   `test_atTheLaunchLimitsAMintOverAnOrphanRoundsAtOneSharesValue`) that failed before the fix. The unfixed copy
   fails the saved counterexamples and the fixed one passes them. The design point became the third residual
   (section 6).

## 6. The stated residuals and Revision 27

The spec's section 3.5 states these, each accepted for the founder's self-funded launch and each to be revisited
before other people's money (spec, lines 991-1053; the README's "Exit liveness" and "Vault favour").

1. **S-L1's crowding (R4).** A clipped entry keeps a redeemable remainder, but a holder with a day of price exposure
   and enough capital can still crowd a door's daily allowance; the fresh-share hold raises the cost from nothing to
   a day's exposure on twice the capital. The deeper fix is an allowance shared pro rata among a delivery's requests.
2. **The sliver after an owner's cancel (merge 7b).** A cancel re-mints each entry its owed value in shares, rounded
   down, so an entry owed exactly 0.03 TAO (`MIN_REDEEM_VALUE`) can come back worth just under it, which every door
   then defers. It is under 0.03 TAO, arises only after an owner's cancel and stays with its holder: `redeemDirect`
   pays any amount.
3. **The orphan at a supply of 0 (the long run).** The last holder's consented floor-price exit while stake is
   missing forfeits its share of the missing stake to holders who stay; with none left, the stake, once relisted,
   sits at a supply of 0 under the virtual offset's shares (42.7 and 49 TAO in the two counterexamples). Nothing on
   chain releases it; it counts in `totalAssets` (using up `depositDirect`'s cap) and in the outflow base, keeps
   earning, and makes every later mint round away up to the orphan / 1e6 in the vault's favour. The treasury's
   permanent share holding (runbook 3.4) keeps the supply above 0, so the forfeit goes to the treasury and the other
   holders instead, from where it can be returned off chain.
4. **The `addHotkey` stayer (the round 2 review).** Outside that corner, a consenting leaver's forfeit goes to
   whoever stays, so a stayer who calls `addHotkey` right after another holder's `acceptMissing` exit takes that
   holder's forfeit. The guard, relisting the hotkey before any nudge toward the floor price, is a carry for the site
   and the runbook, needed before other people's money.
5. **A leaver's forfeit vests to whoever holds (the rerun).** A leaver is paid at a price that leaves out rewards
   not yet vested or claimed, and a door leaver's queued payout stays staked, earning for the holders, until it is
   paid. All of it vests to whoever holds; a claim restarts the schedule over 30 days whatever the stake did since,
   so after a large exit the schedule vests faster than the smaller stake accrues, and a timed entrant gets more than
   its accrual share: at most its share × the departed stake's last vesting period of net rewards (about 1.5% of the
   departed stake at 18% a year), from the stayers' windfall. No principal is at risk, and the leaver ends worse off
   than if it had stayed. The cure is per-holder reward accounting (a rewards-per-share accumulator).
6. **What remains of the fifth, inside the adoption drip.** An adoption still re-spreads what is left of the drip
   over the longer of `rewardVesting` and its own remainder, so anyone (7 days after the last adoption, with a
   surplus of at least the chain's minimum stake, about 0.002 TAO) can push up to a quarter of the drip later, where
   a timed entrant shares it. It is bounded and touches no claimed reward, but the drip can hold stake that came back
   after a write-off: the former holders' principal.

**Revision 27, the adoption drip (fixed the adoption restart).** Fix round 4 found that `adoptStake` restarted the
one reward schedule over max(`rewardVesting`, what was left) without claiming, so rewards holders had already
earned were pushed later and a timed entrant took a share of them: per adoption at most its share × the rewards
left × the time since the last restart / the period, up to a quarter of the schedule, and anyone could adopt after
7 days with a gift of about 0.002 TAO. It was stated as a fifth residual (7f397e0) and then, at the founder's
decision, fixed before launch, since the contracts are immutable. The vault gained a second schedule
(`adoptVestingAmount`, slot 45; `adoptVestingStart` and `adoptVestingPeriod`, slot 46; appended last, so nothing
else moved): `adoptStake` restarts only the drip, a claim carries only its own remainder, `unvestedRewards()` is the
sum of both, and the write-down at a supply of 0 covers both. No event and no published ABI entry changed; the vault
grew 222 B and its lens 215 B. The tests: `VaultAdoptionDrip.t.sol`, the write-down pins and fuzz in
`Supply0Underwater`, the ported adoption tests; the favour campaign asserts its premise (`adoptionsMovedClaims ==
0`) and the core campaign counts an adoption that moves the claim schedule as unexpected. Seeds 8111, 8222 and 8333
of the favour, core and ledger campaigns pass with no vest above accrual left unfunded, and both mutations that
restore the old behaviour fail (section 5). The review approved it; its six minors were fixed (the delivery path's

## 8. The 32-seed sweeps (seeds 101-132, 128 × 64, parity)

`script/seed-sweep.sh 128 64 <log dir> --seeds-file seeds32.txt`, seeds 101 to 132. Each seed is one
`forge test --match-path "test/invariants/*" --fuzz-seed <seed>` at 128 runs × 64 depth under the parity profile,
with its own failure folder, so a saved failure never replays into a later seed. `CASCADE_MIN_HITS_RUNS` is unset,
so B9's minimums are logged, not asserted. A seed is about 17 to 21 minutes on 10 threads.

### 8.1 The first sweep, on 0f07933 (stopped at 14 seeds)

Started on 2026-11-13 at 15:06. Seeds 101 to 111, 113 and 114 passed 305 of 305 (1,051 to 1,184 s each). **Seed 112
failed one invariant:** `ExitsAtLaunchLimitsInvariantTest.invariant_everyClaimHasANonOwnerExit`, "money left with a
path open" (a share holding of the first user, under the floor-raised scenario). The sweep was then stopped, because
Revision 28 (section 1) was about to change the source and the sweep had to run on the final code.

**Seed 112: a test-model gap.** The `crowdTheAllowance` draw's top-up left the user about 1 rao of shares. Her
direct exit was refused `ZeroAmount` in all four rounds of the routine, correctly, since the holding was worth under
1 rao (`CascadeVault.sol`, `redeemDirect`'s zero check). The routine's last delivery then rounded another holder's
burn in the holders' favour and tipped her holding to exactly 1 rao, after the routine's last direct exits. The
judge reads the end state, where her direct path was open (her `redeemDirect` there pays 1e9 wei and leaves 0
shares). No money was stuck. **Fix** (36a85d1): a last pass of direct exits after the routine's last delivery (up to
three passes), a receipt allowance of (`LAST_ROUND` + 1) × the shortfall tolerance for them, and a pin that fails
without the fix. The judge was not weakened: money left with a path open still fails.

**Seed 115:** `CopiedKeyWalkTest.invariant_coWalkNothingExecutes`, "CO: an item the thief scheduled executed:
1 != 0", in the campaign's second run, shrunk to 8 calls: `relayOutage`, `thiefSchedule`, `relayOutage`,
`walkWarp`, `thiefSchedule`, `relayOutage`, `thiefInstrument`, `thiefExecute`.

**What happened** (T0 is the walk's first stamp; the stack stamps every 12 hours):

1. The relayer went down for the longest outage the handler draws, 36 hours less a minute, after relaying the
   stamp as of T0 to both targets.
2. At T0 + 12 h the thief scheduled `setOracle(stranger)` on the front door: a Param item, ready at T0 + 60 h.
3. A second longest outage was drawn while the first was running. `relayOutage` set the return unconditionally,
   so it moved from T0 + 36 h - 1 min to T0 + 48 h - 1 min.
4. A third was drawn at T0 + 36 h, moving the return to T0 + 72 h - 1 min. (The thief's other item, a bridge
   activation, was a decoy it cancelled.)
5. At T0 + 60 h the thief executed `setOracle`. The front door's owner clock was still T0, with no stamp relayed
   since, so the gate passed, as the handler's own model predicted.

**The contract is right.** The spec's copied-key safety (section 3.6) rests on the founder's stamp being relayed
from any funded account within the delay ("relays it from any funded account within the delay (48 h, 7 d or 8 d),
and funds stay safe only if the founder does"). Here no stamp was relayed for 60 hours against a 48-hour item, and
with the thief holding both keys no on-chain rule can tell that item from a legitimate one. The handler's own stated
bound is an outage of at most 36 hours less a minute (`CopiedKeyHandler.sol`, `outageUntil`'s comment): the budget
the 12-hour stack keeps against the 48-hour Param delay, as the Task 39b re-review asked. Chaining outages broke that
bound. Revision 28 did not cause it: the chaining dates from Task 42 (be19097); the item ran exactly at its readyAt,
so the both-windows lapse played no part; and every warp rolls blocks at twice the rate the block floor needs. The
earlier sweeps had not drawn the sequence; Revision 28's new bytecode changed every seed's sequences.

**Fix** (b88b44e, tests only): an outage drawn while the relayer is down now leaves its return as it is
(`outagesWhileDown` counts such draws), so every outage starts with every due stamp relayed and lasts at most 36
hours less a minute; the first stamp at or after an item's scheduling is then relayed, or a newer one is, by its
scheduling + 48 hours - 1 minute, before its readyAt. Two regression tests:
`test_walkEdge_anOutageDrawnWhileDownLeavesItsReturn` (the directed edge) and
`test_walkEdge_seed115ChainedOutagesNoLonger` (the 8 calls replayed: nothing the thief scheduled executes, and its
item dies to the stack).

**The evidence**, all on 7f3dcee's source with only the test files changed:

- Unfixed, a scripted replay of the 8 calls logging every clock fails with the clocks as described above (the
  return at T0 + 36 h, 48 h and 72 h, less a minute each; the execute at T0 + 60 h, the door's owner clock at T0),
  and forge's own replay of the saved counterexample fails the same way.
- Fixed, the scripted replay passes, the walk's 7 edge tests pass (the 2 new among them), and the whole CopiedKey
  file at seed 115, 128 × 64, passes 14 of 14. Its reach stays in the other seeds' range: 769 runs counted, 1,538
  relays after an outage, 658 stale stamps skipped, 5,226 thief items killed by the stack (the 31 passing seeds:
  1,171 to 1,841 relays, 521 to 958 skipped, 3,813 to 6,105 killed).
- **The CopiedKey campaign alone at every sweep seed** (101 to 132, 128 × 64) with the fix, on b88b44e
  (2026-11-14, 16:23 to 18:00): **all 32 seeds passed 14 of 14** (153 to 258 s a seed). Each seed's reach: 709 to
  758 runs counted, 1,351 to 1,589 relays after an outage, 629 to 861 stale stamps skipped, 4,352 to 5,354 thief
  items killed by the stack; outages drawn while the relayer was down are now counted, not chained. These
  ranges lie inside the final sweep's copied-key ranges for the 31 passing seeds (above), so the walk lost no

**Why the other 31 seeds stand:** the change touches only `CopiedKeyHandler.sol` and `CopiedKey.invariants.t.sol`,
and only that test file uses the handler, so every other campaign's bytecode and call sequences are unchanged, as
