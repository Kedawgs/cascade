# CCIP Delivers an IOU, Not Native TAO

Cascade v2 should keep Chainlink CCIP as its only money rail, Base as its first front-door chain, ForeverMoney's bridged TAO as the token users deposit and are paid in, a self-hosted keeper bot on both chains, and an onramp that buys USDC on Base and swaps it inside the app. Nothing else reaches Bittensor EVM today: Hyperlane has no TAO route, LayerZero lists no tokens, and Wormhole and Axelar do not support chain 964. Three assumptions in the current v2 spec would break it on day one, though. First, **CCIP does not deliver native TAO.** It releases ForeverMoney's own ERC-20 claim token, which has no unwrap function. As far as the chain shows, only ForeverMoney's unverified gateway contracts can turn that token into TAO the vault can stake, and CCIP's receive function cannot carry `msg.value` at all. Second, **a contract that claims root rewards must set a gas limit of about 53 million gas, roughly 71% of a block, even when nothing is owed.** Claiming before every price read would therefore use up the ~7.2M gas a CCIP delivery can buy and revert every batch. Third, **a full batch of 100 deposits and 100 redeems probably exceeds that 7.2M ceiling**, and any batch above 5,000 TAO fails at a token pool whose limits ForeverMoney's 2-of-3 Safe sets and every ForeverMoney user shares. Cost is not the problem: an inbound batch costs about $2–11, an outbound message under $0.50, and a user a few cents to a few dollars per cycle. The real risks are dependency and liveness (whether money keeps moving). ForeverMoney, Chainlink and Bittensor's sudo key can each change the rails with no delay. Base holds only about $450k of TAO liquidity. And the spec's 20%-per-day outflow cap, measured against assets net of pending payouts, can strand a large redemption batch indefinitely. The vault's custody model matches the only live precedent and needs targeted fixes, not a rewrite. The first step is to get ForeverMoney's contract-level redeem interface, or its audited source, and to measure delivery gas on a fork of the chain.

## Only CCIP carries TAO to chain 964, and what arrives is an IOU

### One rail moves value, and two others carry only messages

Four general-purpose bridges could in principle connect a vault on Bittensor EVM to Base, but only one moves TAO today. Chainlink's CCIP has been live on Bittensor EVM since **4 November 2025** ([Chainlink changelog](https://dev.chain.link/changelog/ccip-on-bittensor-evm)). Its router there accepts messages to and from Base, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Robinhood Chain and Solana, but not Polygon ([Bittensor router](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xD941fBEcD2b971d0F54b4C34286C95faB52B60B8)). The lane is in real use: by 21 October the Bittensor-side receiving contract had handled about 4,100 messages from Base, and the Base side about 4,250 from Bittensor ([Bittensor OffRamp](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0x51a6150400ed9F0Ae240F5D1b15E3b45Fc4339C7); [Base OffRamp](https://basescan.org/address/0xf09AFe78d3c7d359b334d7cB88995751F7eC5E13)). CCIP's "programmable token transfers" carry one token plus arbitrary data in a single message, and the receiving contract gets both in one transaction ([Chainlink tutorial](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/tutorials/evm/programmable-token-transfers)). That is exactly the shape of v2's `BATCH` and `PAYOUTS` messages.

The alternatives carry messages but no TAO. Hyperlane's core contracts are deployed on Bittensor EVM, but the only token route registered there is a thin USDC route ([Hyperlane registry](https://github.com/hyperlane-xyz/hyperlane-registry/blob/main/deployments/warp_routes/USDC/subtensor-config.yaml)). LayerZero v2 has an endpoint and six verifier networks (DVNs) on the chain but lists zero tokens ([LayerZero deployments](https://metadata.layerzero-api.com/v1/metadata/deployments); [LayerZero DVNs](https://metadata.layerzero-api.com/v1/metadata/dvns)). Neither Wormhole's nor Axelar's supported-chain list includes chain 964 ([Wormhole](https://wormhole.com/docs/reference/supported-networks/); [Axelarscan](https://api.axelarscan.io/api/getChains)).

| Rail | Moves TAO to/from chain 964? | Token and data in one delivery? | Who verifies messages | Inbound batch cost, 21 Oct | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CCIP with ForeverMoney's TAO | Yes, as ForeverMoney's ERC-20 | Yes: one token plus up to ~32 KB | Chainlink's network; the extra Risk Management check is switched off on these lanes | ~$2–11 in, ~$0.3–0.5 out | **Use** |
| CCIP with a Cascade-issued token | Yes | Yes | Same, with Cascade as pool owner | Same, plus a bridge-grade audit | Avoid in v2 |
| Hyperlane, default verifiers | No TAO route (USDC only) | Value only through a route you deploy, with no data callback | 3-of-5 validators (Base→964), 3-of-4 (964→Base) | ~$11 for 3M gas from Base | Messaging fallback only |
| LayerZero v2 | No tokens | Messages only | DVNs you choose (six live) | Not quoted | Possible second check, later |
| Wormhole, Axelar | Not on 964 | n/a | n/a | n/a | Out |

### What CCIP releases on Bittensor is ForeverMoney's token, not TAO

The only "TAO" registered with CCIP on Bittensor EVM is `0xC5b6…0256`, a mintable ERC-20 whose CCIP administrator is ForeverMoney's 2-of-3 Safe. Think of it as an IOU: a token that stands for TAO held somewhere else. It has no `deposit()` or `withdraw()` function, holds zero native TAO, is not source-verified, and 11,057.53 of its 11,057.58 tokens sit locked in the CCIP pool ([token on explorer](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xC5b6C1632d34901239396F5E1BDe54B342900256); [pool on explorer](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xB66b9c085ab711ECC373aaF6dc959a4A6B42BE2e); [CCIP token directory](https://docs.chain.link/api/ccip/v1/tokens?environment=mainnet&symbol=TAO)). The TAO behind it is staked in ForeverMoney's own vault on Bittensor; going from Base to Bittensor, "the EVM token is burned before the corresponding asset is released or delivered on Finney" ([ForeverMoney bridge docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/bridge)). The chain's canonical wrapped TAO (`0x5F3B…3522`, a verified WETH9 clone that does unwrap) has no CCIP pool, and Cascade cannot register one for it. Chainlink's self-service registration requires an `owner()` or `getCCIPAdmin()` function, and this contract has neither ([WTAO on explorer](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0x5F3B70e0c089a1e3020B1990823Bc241a7bF3522); [CCIP registration docs](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/concepts/cross-chain-token/evm/registration-administration)).

The receiving side has a second mismatch. CCIP's `ccipReceive` function is not `payable`, so `msg.value` is always zero. Tokens are transferred to the receiver as an ERC-20 just before the call ([CCIPReceiver API](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/api-reference/evm/v1.6.0/ccip-receiver)). The v2 interface `onBridgeMessage(…, amount) payable` with the check `msg.value == amount` therefore cannot be fed by CCIP directly. A real delivery on 21 October shows what ForeverMoney does with its own traffic. Its receiver took 2.98 of these tokens from the pool, burned them, and triggered its vault, all in the same transaction and using 474,968 gas ([delivery transaction](https://evm.taostats.io/tx/0x306aae48f1c794ee10bf5734d8d8cd2b8b292123fdb7f8bbf7473798f2de91be)). ForeverMoney has already built and runs the exact receiver Cascade needs, and Hackerbane audited its bridge contracts in September 2026 with no critical, high or medium findings ([Hackerbane report](https://hackerbane.com/reports/hackerbane-HACK-AR-2026-forevermoney-bridge.html)). Its gateway's source is not published on the explorer, though, and its docs describe only app and SDK integration, not a function other contracts can call ([ForeverMoney docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/llms-full.txt)).

That leaves three ways to get stakeable TAO into the vault. **Path A, conversion through ForeverMoney, is the one to pursue.** Cascade's Bittensor adapter would receive the token and the batch together, call a ForeverMoney function that swaps the token for native TAO in the same transaction, and then call the vault with real value. Atomic delivery and the vault's `msg.value` check would both survive. It needs ForeverMoney's agreement and a documented interface. **Path B, a Cascade-issued CCIP token wrapping native TAO, is clean on paper but wrong for v2.** It makes Cascade a bridge-token issuer, with pool ownership, rate-limit administration and audits that cost bridge-level money ($150k or more; [Zealynx](https://www.zealynx.io/research/audit-ops/audit-pricing-2026)). It also cannot work on the deposit side. A Cascade token on Base would exist only after someone bridged TAO out of Bittensor, so users who bought ForeverMoney's TAO with USDC would hold nothing the front door could send (my inference from the lock-and-mint design). **Path C, two separate messages, is the fallback.** The front door sends ForeverMoney's TAO through ForeverMoney's own route with "liquid" delivery to the vault's Bittensor address, and the batch data travels separately as a CCIP data-only message. That works with no new ForeverMoney code, but delivery stops being atomic. The vault must then hold each batch as "awaiting funds" and credit it only against balance it can attribute to that batch, never staking unattributed balance in the meantime. Because strangers can also send TAO to the same address, that attribution is fragile. ForeverMoney's alternative "staked" delivery option ([ForeverMoney bridge docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/bridge)) is undocumented on-chain, and stake transfers are "NOT exact" according to ForeverMoney's own field notes ([AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol)).

The payout direction has the same constraint in reverse. The vault cannot lock native TAO into ForeverMoney's pool, so it must hand TAO to ForeverMoney's Bittensor gateway, which mints the Base token to the front door. If that gateway cannot carry Cascade's payout list, the list travels as a separate CCIP data-only message (about $0.16 at 3M gas). The front door's existing `TokensNotReceived` check already handles that case. The data message fails until the tokens land, and then anyone can re-run it. ForeverMoney's own example of a 0.1-TAO Bittensor-to-Base transfer cost $0.25 in CCIP fees plus $0.94 of gas on the sending side ([ForeverMoney fees](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/bridge/fees)).

### The rail's controls sit with ForeverMoney's signers and are mid-upgrade at Chainlink

Using this rail means trusting three outside parties, none of whom is subject to Cascade's 7-day delay. **ForeverMoney's 2-of-3 Safe, with the same three owners on both chains, owns the token pools, administers the token's CCIP registration, and sets the Base rate limits.** Those powers let it change limits, swap the pool, or set it to zero, which removes the token from CCIP. The registry-administrator and Base rate-limit roles moved from single keys to that Safe only on **1 October 2026** ([Safe on Bittensor](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xADF60fcC63217961c931d57f1dCE2C5d70Af3546); [Base pool](https://basescan.org/address/0xF5AC6D6Bd12d8b87dE3c136FAc9375961577e794); [ForeverMoney docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/llms-full.txt)). On Chainlink's side, the older (version 1.6) receiving contracts on both Bittensor lanes report that Risk Management Network verification, an extra independent check, is disabled. The lanes are also mid-migration to CCIP 2.0, whose default verifier is "a decentralized network of 16 independent node operators" ([Bittensor OffRamp](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0x51a6150400ed9F0Ae240F5D1b15E3b45Fc4339C7); [CCIP architecture](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/concepts/architecture/overview)). ForeverMoney's proof of reserves showed **100.07%** backing on 20 October. The page itself calls this "an on-chain balance comparison, not an independent audit," it leaves unclaimed rewards out, and its API returned an error the next day ([ForeverMoney proof-of-reserves docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/proof-of-reserves); [PoR API](https://forevermoney.ai/api/proof-of-reserves)).

### Delivery rules: atomic, retryable, size-capped and rate-limited

The delivery rules are mostly friendly to a vault. Tokens are released first, then the receiver runs. If the receiver reverts, the whole delivery reverts, nothing is lost, and the message enters a `FAILURE` state that anyone can re-execute with a higher (never lower) gas limit. A message that has succeeded cannot run twice ([OffRamp source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smartcontractkit/chainlink-ccip/contracts-ccip-v1.6.0/chains/evm/contracts/offRamp/OffRamp.sol); [CCIP manual execution](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/concepts/manual-execution)). On these lanes, permissionless re-execution opens after one hour ([Bittensor OffRamp](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0x51a6150400ed9F0Ae240F5D1b15E3b45Fc4339C7)). Each message carries one token and under 32,000 bytes of data. Delivery gas has a hard ceiling. For Base→Bittensor, an 8,000,000-gas total includes 300,000 of overhead, 90,000 for the token and 20 gas per byte of data, which left about **7.2M gas for the receiver** on a 7 KB batch; 7.5M was rejected. Bittensor→Base accepted 13M ([Base FeeQuoter](https://basescan.org/address/0x0352750e6e37e3af39E314D26EB75b950eB3Cb62); [Base router](https://basescan.org/address/0x881e3A65B4d4a04dD529061dd0071cf975F58bCD)). The gas you buy is priced into the fee and not refunded ([CCIP best practices](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/concepts/best-practices/evm)).

Rate limits are the second ceiling. Both ForeverMoney pools cap each direction at **5,000 TAO (about $1.35M)**, refilling at 0.6 TAO per second, or 51,840 TAO a day and about 2.3 hours from empty to full ([pool on explorer](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xB66b9c085ab711ECC373aaF6dc959a4A6B42BE2e)). That allowance is shared with every ForeverMoney user. A delivery that hits the limit on the receiving side fails with `TokenHandlingError`, and Chainlink notes that "raising `gasLimitOverride` will not help" ([CCIP manual execution](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/concepts/manual-execution)).

Fees are small and set mostly by the Bittensor-side gas you buy, as live quotes from 21 October show ([Base router](https://basescan.org/address/0x881e3A65B4d4a04dD529061dd0071cf975F58bCD); [Bittensor router](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xD941fBEcD2b971d0F54b4C34286C95faB52B60B8)):

| Message | Delivery gas bought | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Base→Bittensor, token only | 0 | $0.78 |
| Base→Bittensor, token + 10 entries | 500k | $1.49 |
| Base→Bittensor, token + 100 entries | 1M | $2.33 |
| Base→Bittensor, token + 100 entries | 3M | $5.06 |
| Base→Bittensor, token + 7 KB | 7.2M (lane maximum) | $10.69 |
| Bittensor→Base, token only | 0 | $0.26 |
| Bittensor→Base, token + 100 payouts | 3M | $0.32 |
| Bittensor→Base, token + 7 KB | 13M | $0.49 |

Because unused gas is not refunded, buying 3M instead of 1M adds $2.70 to every inbound batch, so the keeper should size the gas limit from each batch's entry count. Running `processPayouts` itself on Bittensor costs another $1.40–4.10 for 1–3M gas at the chain's 5-gwei floor price ([payout quotes and gas reads](https://lite.chain.opentensor.ai)).

### Hyperlane and LayerZero are fallbacks for data, not money

Hyperlane's default verifier sets are respectable: three of five named validators (Abacus Works, Zee Prime, Substance Labs, Luganodes, Enigma) for messages into Bittensor, and three of four (Abacus Works, Zee Prime, Mitosis, Merkly) for messages out, each behind a pause switch ([Hyperlane ISM defaults](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hyperlane-xyz/hyperlane-monorepo/main/typescript/sdk/src/consts/multisigIsm.ts); [Bittensor Mailbox](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xF767D698c510FE5E53b46BA6Fd1174F5271e390A)). Moving TAO over Hyperlane, though, would mean deploying and owning a warp route (Hyperlane's term for a token bridge route), and stock native routes pay the recipient a bare transfer with "no data or callback" ([TokenRouter.sol](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hyperlane-xyz/hyperlane-monorepo/main/solidity/contracts/token/libs/TokenRouter.sol)). Routes deployed with Hyperlane's command-line tool also start with a single trusted-relayer verifier that must be removed before production ([Hyperlane docs](https://docs.hyperlane.xyz/docs/guides/production/warp-route-deployment/remove-trusted-relayer.md)). Autheo's Hyperlane bridge lost about **$1.76M on Base on 12–13 October 2026**, which monitors attribute to exactly that setup; Autheo has not confirmed the cause ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/autheo-s-theo-plunges-92-after-suspected-bridge-exploit-on-base); [Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/13/autheo-theo-crashes-over-90-after-1-7m-hyperlane-bridge-exploit-on-base/)). A claimed critical warp-route bug report (issue #8589) was closed as "not planned" with no public comment ([hyperlane-monorepo #8589](https://github.com/hyperlane-xyz/hyperlane-monorepo/issues/8589)). Hyperlane is also roughly twice CCIP's price for an inbound batch, at about $11 for 3M gas from Base ([Base IGP](https://basescan.org/address/0xc3F23848Ed2e04C0c6d41bd7804fa8f89F940B94)). LayerZero could later add an independent second check on batch data, but Kelp DAO's **$292M** loss after it dropped to a single verifier shows what a lax configuration costs ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/layerzero-s-incident-report-says-kelp-downgraded-from-2-of-2-to-1-of-1-before-usd292m-exploit)). Neither belongs in v2.

## Base is the only working front door, and $450k of liquidity caps every exit

### ForeverMoney's TAO exists only on Base and Robinhood Chain

On Base, the asset to escrow and pay out is ForeverMoney's TAO, `0xf308…3d67`. It is an 18-decimal ERC-20 in Chainlink's burn-and-mint style, and only the CCIP pool can mint or burn it. Its administrator is the same 2-of-3 Safe. It is not an upgradeable proxy, has no EIP-2612 permit (sign-to-approve), and had about 7,025 tokens outstanding on 21 October ([Base token](https://basescan.org/address/0xf3081494B87e8D5fb7960f066E931D1D0e6E3d67); [Base pool](https://basescan.org/address/0xF5AC6D6Bd12d8b87dE3c136FAc9375961577e794)). The same token address is used on Robinhood Chain ([ForeverMoney docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/llms-full.txt)). The front door should hard-code this address. An unrelated "TAO Bittensor" token with a billion-token supply also trades on Base ([DexScreener](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/tokens/0x9389147397FE4F0CB25d656E2FB6E312bF733D46)).

The plan for "later Ethereum, BNB, Polygon" front doors does not survive contact with the token map. Chainlink's router has lanes to Ethereum and BNB Chain, but ForeverMoney's pool supports only Base and Robinhood Chain, and its Ethereum limit is set to zero ([Bittensor pool](https://evm.taostats.io/address/0xB66b9c085ab711ECC373aaF6dc959a4A6B42BE2e)). Polygon has no lane at all.

### Pay out ForeverMoney's TAO, and let the app swap it

ForeverMoney's TAO on Base is the right payout token because it is the deposit token, a plain ERC-20, and the only deep TAO market reachable from Base. The alternatives fail. Paying out USDC would need a swap venue on Bittensor EVM, where all of DeFi held about $30k on 21 October ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/v2/chains)). A wrapped csTAO on Base would make Cascade a token issuer (Path B above). The downside is that users hold ForeverMoney's IOU between payout and swap, and it earns nothing: ForeverMoney's reserve calculation excludes unclaimed rewards, so staking yield on the backing stays with ForeverMoney ([ForeverMoney proof-of-reserves docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/proof-of-reserves)). Deposits waiting in an open batch and payouts sitting in `claimable` therefore earn nothing. That argues for shorter batch intervals and prompt claims.

Swap costs at retail sizes are low, but depth runs out quickly. KyberSwap quotes on 21 October showed these losses against the reference price ([KyberSwap buy route](https://aggregator-api.kyberswap.com/base/api/v1/routes?tokenIn=0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913&tokenOut=0xf3081494B87e8D5fb7960f066E931D1D0e6E3d67&amountIn=50000000000); [KyberSwap sell route](https://aggregator-api.kyberswap.com/base/api/v1/routes?tokenIn=0xf3081494B87e8D5fb7960f066E931D1D0e6E3d67&tokenOut=0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913&amountIn=183000000000000000000)):

|---|---|---|
| ~$500 | 0.31% | 0.30% |
| ~$5,000 | 0.45% | 0.42% |

Larger fills depend on private market makers, which may not quote for contract-initiated swaps. Route through KyberSwap or ParaSwap directly. LI.FI adds a 0.25% fee and found worse sell routes ([LI.FI quote](https://li.quest/v1/quote?fromChain=8453&toChain=8453&fromToken=0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913&toToken=0xf3081494B87e8D5fb7960f066E931D1D0e6E3d67&fromAmount=5000000000&fromAddress=0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD)).

This table, not the vault's parameters, sets the real exit capacity. For a $250k beta, a 20% day of redemptions is $50k, which Base can absorb at about 2% slippage if everyone sells the same day. At $5M, the same cap would allow $1M a day, more than twice the two Base pools' combined depth. TVL caps should therefore follow ForeverMoney TAO liquidity on Base and Robinhood Chain.

### Card dollars arrive as USDC, and onramp fees dwarf everything else

Coinbase cannot put TAO on Base. Its TAO listing accepts only native Bittensor addresses ([Coinbase currencies API](https://api.coinbase.com/v2/currencies/crypto)), so "buy TAO" in the app means buying USDC on Base and swapping it. Coinbase's hosted guest checkout was **deprecated on 30 June 2026**. Its replacement, the Headless Onramp API, takes Apple Pay or Google Pay only, works in the US only, caps purchases at $2.5k a week, and requires app approval plus a verified phone number and email ([Coinbase onramp overview](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/onramp-overview); [Coinbase onramp FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq.md); [create-order API](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/v2/rest-api/onramp/create-an-onramp-order)). Fees are 2.5% for credit cards and 0.5% for bank (ACH) transfers, plus a price spread. Zero-fee USDC is reserved for selected partners ([Coinbase onramp FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq.md)), and one comparison puts Apple Pay or Google Pay without a Coinbase account at about 3.99% ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15210390-best-stablecoin-onramps-2026-moonpay-transak-coinbase-onramp-compared)). For a $500 card user that adds up to roughly **2.9% plus spread, or about $14–15**: about $12.50 for the onramp, $1.50 for the swap, and cents for gas and the user's share of bridge fees. The user's own steps take seconds. Shares arrive only after the batch closes and CCIP delivers, and neither Apple Pay's delivery time nor CCIP's transit time on this lane is documented.

The deposit itself can take one signature. ForeverMoney's TAO has no permit, so the app should bundle approve, swap and deposit as one EIP-5792 batch in wallets that support it, such as Coinbase Smart Wallet and MetaMask after its EIP-7702 upgrade ([MetaMask docs](https://docs.metamask.io/metamask-connect/evm/guides/send-transactions/batch-transactions.md)). The user then stays `msg.sender`, and the spec does not change. A "zap" contract for other wallets would need a `depositFor(user)` entry on the front door. That breaks the spec's "always `msg.sender`" rule, but it is safe if limited to deposits, since it can only give shares to the named user.

Wallet choice creates a tension the spec only half-acknowledges. The vault credits shares on Bittensor to the user's Base address. The research found no sign that Coinbase Smart Wallet's factory is deployed on chain 964, so shares minted to such an address may be unreachable there. Even a user with a plain wallet address needs native TAO on chain 964 to pay gas for `redeemDirect`. The research found no faucet or gas sponsorship for the chain; the documented ways to get gas start from a native Bittensor wallet ([Bittensor docs: fund an EVM key](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/tx/fund-evm-key); [vTAO bridge tutorial](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/evm-tutorials/vtao-bridge-tutorial)). The direct "escape hatch" is a real exit for Bittensor-native users and a theoretical one for the USDC-on-Base users Cascade is built for. Letting users register a separate Bittensor recovery address through the front door would close most of that gap.

## Bittensor EVM breaks a dozen plain-Ethereum assumptions

The closest precedent is ForeverMoney's AlphaVault: one contract on chain 964 holding about **11,065 TAO of root stake on one validator hotkey**, using its own mirror address as the coldkey (the owner key of a stake position), with no proxies. That is the custody model v2 plans ([bridge-contracts README](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts); [AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol)). Its code comments are the best field notes available, and most of the fixes below borrow from them. Nothing found suggests anyone has yet deployed a CCIP receiver on Bittensor that mints balances for many users in one message, so Cascade's batch receiver would be a first. CCIP holds only about $3.4M on the chain ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/v2/chains)).

### Claiming rewards needs a 53-million-gas allowance and can be refused

Root rewards accrue every epoch into the validator's basket fund (a pool of subnet tokens the validator manages), and stakers realise them only by claiming. A claim converts the entitlement into **more root stake on the same hotkey**, not into spendable balance ([Root Reborn docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/root-reborn)). Basket trading is switched on for mainnet, even though the docs page still says testnet only ([Release v468](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/releases/tag/v468); [pallet storage](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/lib.rs); [basket trading docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/basket-trading-for-stakers)). The claim precompile (Bittensor's built-in contract for staking) declares its worst-case workload up front and checks that workload against the caller's gas limit before doing anything. As a result, `claimRootWithHotkey` needed a gas limit of **53,211,036 even with nothing to claim**, and `claimRoot` needed 64.9M, against a 75M-gas block ([extensions.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/precompiles/src/extensions.rs); [claim_root.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/claim_root.rs); [live estimate](https://archive.chain.opentensor.ai)).

How the call fails matters too. Precompile runtime failures surface as low-level errors with no revert data, and the EVM engine Bittensor uses discards all the gas forwarded to the call ([precompile error path](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/precompiles/src/extensions.rs); [SputnikVM executor](https://docs.rs/crate/evm/0.43.4/source/src/executor/stack/executor.rs)). Solidity's `try/catch` takes the catch branch, but the caller is left with about 1/64 of its gas. Inside a CCIP delivery capped near 7.2M gas, the spec's `try STAKING.claimRootWithHotkey(hotkey) {} catch {}` would fail its gas check, burn about 7.1M gas, and leave the rest of the batch to run out of gas. The delivery reverts, and no retry can fix it, because the lane cannot buy 53M gas. This conclusion comes from the engine's source code and has not yet been tested on-chain. Capping the gas forwarded to the claim avoids the burn but guarantees the claim never succeeds inline.

Claims can also be refused for reasons outside the vault's control. On 21 October, ForeverMoney's 11,064.78-TAO position had 55.67 TAO (0.50%) unclaimed, and both claim paths failed with `RootClaimTooHeavy`. That error fires when the hotkey's basket has more than 129 data rows to process, legacy duplicates included ([claim_root.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/claim_root.rs); [live probe](https://archive.chain.opentensor.ai)). No source says when such a position clears. A claim also costs real money, about **τ0.057 (~$15) for a full 128-holding basket** ([Root Reborn docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/root-reborn)). It sells basket holdings at pool prices with no slippage bound. ForeverMoney restricted claims to an operator for that reason, writing that leaving them permissionless "was wrong" ([AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol)). Reading the unclaimed amount on-chain costs 1.7M gas per call, which rules out folding it into the share price.

### Staking floors, rao rounding and silent sweeps

Root unstaking is 1:1, fee-free, and credited inside the same call, so the vault's balance rises by exactly the requested amount times 10⁹ wei ([stake_utils.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/stake_utils.rs)). The edges are where things go wrong. Asking to unstake more than the position **silently exits the whole position** instead of reverting. Partial unstakes below 0.002 TAO revert. Leaving a remainder below **0.02 TAO** on a hotkey the vault does not own triggers an automatic sweep of the whole remainder into the vault's balance, with no fee ([remove_stake.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/remove_stake.rs); [helpers.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/helpers.rs)). Both floors are governance-set and readable on-chain. A brand-new position reads back one rao short ([AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol)). Balances are kept in rao (10⁻⁹ TAO), so any transfer amount below 10⁹ wei simply does not move. `msg.value` can therefore overstate what arrived by up to 10⁹−1 wei, and 500 rao is held back once as an existential deposit (the minimum balance an account must keep) ([EVM runner](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/vendor/frontier/frame/evm/src/runner/stack.rs); [Bittensor EVM docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/evm)).

| Operation (from the live ForeverMoney vault, 21 Oct) | Gas |
|---|---|
| `removeStake` (root) | 144,811 |
| `removeStakeFull` | 147,852 |
| `moveStake` root to root | 121,699 |
| `unstakeAll` | 2,499,841 |
| `claimRootWithHotkey` | ~53.2M limit required |
| `getUnclaimedRootTaoByHotkey` (read only) | 1,699,302 |
| `setRejectStakingHotkeyGrowth` | 26,351 |
| `addressMapping` (look up own coldkey) | 22,353 |

At the 5-gwei floor, 1M gas costs 0.005 TAO, about $1.35 ([gas readings](https://archive.chain.opentensor.ai); [runtime config](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/runtime/src/lib.rs)).

### Value arrives without running code, and the rules change weekly

TAO sent to the vault's Bittensor address from a native wallet, and every unstake credit, arrives as a direct balance change that runs no EVM code. A `receive()` that reverts therefore blocks nothing. ForeverMoney opens its `receive()` only around its own precompile calls, so that a future runtime that credited through EVM transfers would not "brick EVERY redemption" ([AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol); [fund-evm-key](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/tx/fund-evm-key)). The `setRejectStakingHotkeyGrowth` flag only stops outsiders from adding new hotkeys to the vault's list. Stake sent onto a hotkey the vault already uses still lands, so the spec's "donations only raise the price" stance is necessary, not optional ([stake_utils.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/stake_utils.rs); [error docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/errors/chain/AccountRejectsStakingHotkeyGrowth)).

The vault can derive its own coldkey on-chain through the `0x…080c` precompile for about 22k gas. ForeverMoney passed it in by hand and ended up with "a duplicate vault storing another vault's coldkey" ([address_mapping.rs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/precompiles/src/address_mapping.rs); [AlphaVault.sol](https://github.com/ForeverMoney-Ai/bridge-contracts/blob/main/src/AlphaVault.sol)). Moving stake to a new hotkey does not claim the old hotkey's basket yield; that yield stays owed and lands on the old hotkey when claimed ([Root Reborn docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/root-reborn)). The optional hold on root exits (`RootStakeUnlockInterval`) is zero today. If governance turns it on, every stake would restart the clock, so staking after every batch could lock the vault's own exits ([pallet storage docs](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/lib.rs); [RootStakeLocked](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/errors/chain/RootStakeLocked)). Root stake operations also pause during basket migrations ([too-early errors](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/errors/too-early)).

Governance is the risk that cannot be coded away. Bittensor's sudo key, held by a Rao Foundation multisig, can disable a precompile with no notice period ([precompile lifecycle](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/evm/precompile-design); [admin-utils](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/admin-utils/src/lib.rs)). **Ten runtime versions shipped between 21 September and 19 October 2026** ([subtensor releases](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/releases)). Some upgrades have changed behaviour under existing function names: `claimRoot` has ignored its argument since v441 ([Root Reborn docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/root-reborn)), and one September fix broke contract deposit flows for a day ([PR #3140](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/pull/3140)). No 2026 upgrade was found that broke a direct precompile call from a plain contract. Pre-releases appear on GitHub days before they reach mainnet, which leaves time for a regression run on testnet 945.

On the operational side, the public "lite" RPC drops state older than about 300 blocks and returned rate-limit errors after about ten rapid calls. Keepers need the archive endpoint or a paid provider (dRPC charges about $6 per million calls) ([Bittensor EVM docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/evm); [Dwellir comparison](https://www.dwellir.com/blog/best-bittensor-rpc-providers)). Blocks arrive every 12 seconds, finality trails the head by about three blocks, tips are ignored, and blocks are nearly empty, so inclusion is immediate at the floor price. Docs recommend compiling with Solidity 0.8.24 for the Cancun EVM version, which v2 already does. Contracts are capped at 24,576 bytes, and ForeverMoney's AlphaVault is 19,129, a useful benchmark for a vault that folds in its staking engine ([Deploy a contract](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/evm/deploy-a-contract); [EVM config](https://github.com/RaoFoundation/subtensor/blob/main/vendor/frontier/frame/evm/src/lib.rs)). Safe v1.4.1 and Multicall3 are deployed on chain 964, which covers the vault's owner and guardian roles ([Safe deployments](https://github.com/safe-global/safe-deployments/blob/main/src/assets/v1.4.1/safe_l2.json)).

## Keepers must be self-hosted, and reward claims need a guard

A keeper is the bot that calls routine contract functions on schedule. The hosted keeper services of 2024–25 are gone, and the one survivor does not reach Bittensor.

| Service | Status, October 2026 | Works on chain 964? | Role for Cascade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainlink Automation | v1.x sunset 30 June, v2.1 sunset 31 July 2026 | n/a | Gone |
| Gelato Web3 Functions | Shut down 31 March 2026 (per a migration vendor) | n/a | Gone |
| OpenZeppelin Defender (hosted) | Retired 1 July 2026 | n/a | Gone |
| Chainlink CRE | Live; deploys in early access; 3 workflows per org; pricing private | No (Base yes) | Optional second trigger on Base |
| OpenZeppelin Relayer (self-hosted) | Open source (AGPL), any EVM chain by RPC, cloud key storage | Yes | Good signing layer |
| Plain self-hosted bot | n/a | Yes | Primary keeper |

Sources: [Chainlink Automation migration](https://docs.chain.link/cre/reference/cla-migration-go.md); [KeeperHub on Gelato](https://docs.keeperhub.com/guides/gelato-migration); [Defender sunset](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/defender-sunset-faq); [CRE networks](https://docs.chain.link/cre/supported-networks-ts); [CRE quotas](https://docs.chain.link/cre/service-quotas); [OpenZeppelin Relayer](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/relayer).

The recommended setup is a self-hosted bot on both chains, with its keys in OpenZeppelin Relayer or a cloud key manager, and CRE as an optional backup caller on Base. CRE's forwarder sends no ETH, so a backup would need a small contract that holds ETH to pay `closeBatch` fees ([CRE Gelato migration](https://docs.chain.link/cre/reference/gelato-migration-ts)). Because `closeBatch`, `processPayouts` and CCIP re-execution are all permissionless, a stolen keeper key can only delay things. The bot needs ETH on Base and TAO on chain 964. The simplest TAO source is an exchange withdrawal to the bot's Bittensor mirror address, since Coinbase sends TAO only to native addresses ([Coinbase currencies API](https://api.coinbase.com/v2/currencies/crypto); [stake-from-EVM guide](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/guides/evm/stake-from-evm)).

The keeper's job is broader than the spec implies. Before closing a batch, it should check that the batch's value fits both pools' current rate-limit allowance, size the delivery gas to the entry count, and pay the ETH fee. Afterwards, it watches for `FAILURE` messages and re-runs them once the limit refills or with more gas. On Bittensor it runs `processPayouts`, stakes idle TAO on a schedule rather than inside deliveries, checks that the staking precompile is still enabled (`getPrecompileStatus`), and tests each Bittensor pre-release on testnet before it ships.

Reward claims need their own policy. Because a claim sells basket holdings with no slippage bound, a permissionless claim lets an attacker push basket token prices down, trigger the vault's claim, and buy back. The loss is bounded by the unclaimed amount, 0.5% in ForeverMoney's case. A practical compromise is to restrict claims to a keeper role that checks the expected payout off-chain against the stake actually added, and to let anyone claim if no claim has happened for, say, seven days. The keeper role can only time claims, never move funds. Claim frequency should follow economics. At the 4–7% yearly root yield earlier sources reported, which is unverified since the October runtime changes ([Staking Rewards](https://www.stakingrewards.com/asset/bittensor)), a $250k vault earns roughly $27–48 a day. A daily full-basket claim at ~$15 would eat a third to over half of that. Weekly claims cost about 4–8% of accrued yield and leave at most a week of rewards (around 0.1%) out of the share price.

Settlement is cheap enough for the keeper to keep paying it, as Lido and Lagoon operators do ([Lido withdrawal queue](https://docs.lido.fi/contracts/withdrawal-queue-erc721); [Lagoon flows](https://docs.lagoon.finance/vault/deposit-and-withdraw-flows)). One full 100-entry deposit batch and one full payout batch a day cost about $7–10, or 7–10 cents per user. At ten entries per batch that rises to 33–60 cents per user, and a lone user costs $1–3 (computed from the fee quotes above). The spec's split of costs still works on CCIP: the keeper pays the inbound fee in ETH, and the outbound fee comes out of the payout pro-rata, since the Bittensor router accepts native TAO as a fee token ([CCIP chain directory](https://docs.chain.link/api/ccip/v1/chains?environment=mainnet&chainId=964)). Because batches cost dollars rather than tens of dollars, and because ForeverMoney's TAO earns nothing while it waits, a 6–12-hour batch interval with close-when-full fits the beta better than the spec's one-day default. Empty batches roll over at no bridge cost.

## Fourteen changes make the v2 spec deployable

The core of v2 survives: accounting next to the stake, one price per batch, pull-based claims, sets of bridge adapters, no in-flight lock, and donations that only raise the price. What must change is mostly where the spec assumed an ordinary EVM and a generic bridge.

| # | v2 spec today | What the chain or bridge actually does | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Native TAO arrives with `msg.value == amount` | CCIP releases ForeverMoney's ERC-20; `ccipReceive` is not payable; only ForeverMoney's contracts unwrap it | Adapter receives token plus batch, converts through a ForeverMoney call in the same transaction, then calls the vault with value; fall back to "awaiting funds" crediting if conversion is asynchronous |
| 2 | Vault pays out with `bridge.send{value}` over CCIP | The vault cannot lock native TAO into ForeverMoney's pool | Payout adapter goes through ForeverMoney's gateway; payout list may travel as a separate data message, which the front door's re-execution already tolerates |
| 3 | `_claimRewards()` in a `try/catch` before every price | Needs a ~53.2M gas limit; failure burns forwarded gas; can be refused; ~τ0.057 per full claim; no slippage bound | Remove from every pricing path; standalone claim that checks `gasleft()`; keeper-timed with a permissionless fallback; claim cadence set by economics |
| 4 | Up to 100 deposits + 100 redeems per message | ~7.2–7.4M usable delivery gas; storing each payout entry costs two fresh storage slots | Store only a hash of each payout list (keeper resubmits the list as calldata); start at 50 + 50; raise after fork measurements; size gas per batch |
| 5 | No per-batch value limit | 5,000-TAO allowance per message direction, 0.6 TAO/s refill, shared with all ForeverMoney users | Cap each batch's deposits below the allowance (e.g. 4,000 TAO); keeper reads both pools' limiters before sending |
| 6 | `_stakeIdle()` inside delivery and after every batch | Precompile failures burn gas; a future root hold would re-arm on every stake | Stake idle TAO in a separate keeper call on a schedule |
| 7 | 20%/day cap vs `totalAssets()`, all-or-nothing per batch | `totalAssets()` excludes pending payouts, so large batches can never clear | Count outflow when shares are burned, against gross assets; defer entries beyond the window instead of parking whole batches |
| 8 | 7-day registry delay as the main safeguard | Covers Cascade's keys only; at most ~79% can exit in 7 days at 20%/day | Let the guardian cancel pending proposals; keep a standby adapter; disclose outside admins |
| 9 | Constructor takes `bytes32 coldkey` | Precompile `0x…080c` derives it; hand-supplied keys caused a ForeverMoney deploy incident | Derive on-chain; keep the parameter only as a cross-check |
| 10 | Owner-set `minStakeRao`, default 0.002 TAO | Live 0.002-TAO minimum plus a 0.02-TAO sweep floor; over-requests exit fully | Read both live; leave at least 0.02 TAO or exit with `removeStakeFull`; measure balance changes around every precompile call |
| 11 | Amounts in 18-decimal wei | Transfers truncate to rao (10⁹ wei) | Front door accepts only whole-rao deposits; vault requires `amount % 1e9 == 0`; round payouts down; keep at least 500 rao free |
| 12 | `receive()` always reverts | Credits bypass it today, but a future runtime could route them through it | Accept value only during the vault's own precompile calls |
| 13 | `setHotkey` claims, then moves; one-hotkey `rootStakeRao()` | Old-hotkey yield stays owed there and lands there when claimed | Track every hotkey with stake; sum them in `totalAssets()`; claim old hotkeys later |
| 14 | Future front doors on Ethereum, BNB, Polygon; same-address shares | No ForeverMoney pools there, no Polygon lane; smart-wallet factories and gas are missing on 964 | Base (plus optionally Robinhood Chain); bridge USDC in-app; add a user-registered Bittensor recovery address |

The ForeverMoney integration (rows 1 and 2) gates everything else and should be settled first. The audited source exists at a named commit ([ForeverMoney docs](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/llms-full.txt)), so the question for ForeverMoney is narrow. Can a third-party contract on Bittensor turn their token into native TAO in one call, and can their Base gateway forward a data payload to a receiver? A yes to both keeps v2's atomic design intact. Even then, the adapter should be built as the part designed to be replaced, because it is where every outside change lands.

Batch sizing (rows 4–6) needs measurement, not guesswork. The research's estimates for 100 deposits alone range from about 3.5M to 5.5M gas. By my own rough count, writing 100 payout entries to storage adds another 4–5M, which would push a full two-sided batch past the ~7.2M ceiling. Chainlink's own guidance is to "validate and record the message, then perform nonessential work separately" ([CCIP billing](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/billing.md)). Recording a hash, minting shares and moving staking out of the delivery path follows that advice and leaves headroom.

The outflow cap (rows 7 and 8) is the one change that protects honest users from the spec itself. `processPayouts` compares a batch's total with 20% of `totalAssets()`, but `totalAssets()` has already subtracted `pendingPayouts`, and the check is all-or-nothing. Take a 1,000-TAO vault where one batch redeems 300 TAO. After the shares are burned, `totalAssets()` reads 700 and the daily allowance is 140, so the 300-TAO batch can never pass unless new deposits grow the vault. In a mass exit the allowance shrinks toward zero as redemptions queue. Even a corrected cap lets at most 1 − 0.8⁷ ≈ 79% of the vault leave within the 7-day registry delay. The promise that users can exit before a bad registry change takes effect therefore only partly holds. Lengthening the delay to 14 days (about 96%) would fix the arithmetic but would also slow the emergency replacement of a broken adapter, which weekly runtime upgrades make a live scenario. A guardian veto over pending proposals is the better fix. Cancelling can only stop flows, so it fits a guardian whose only other power is to pause.

The test doubles need the same realism. `MockStaking` should model clipping, the 0.02-TAO sweep, the one-rao shortfall on new positions, gas-burning failures, the claim allowance and `RootClaimTooHeavy`. `MockBridge` should deliver ERC-20s, not native value, and enforce the 5,000-TAO limit and the gas ceiling. Fork tests against live chain 964 should confirm what the mocks assume.

## Conclusion

The research moves v2's risk from inside the contract to outside it. The spec was written to stop Cascade's own keys from moving user funds, and its registry delay, outflow cap and pause-only guardian are sound in that intent. But the parties who can actually stop or redirect money in this design sit outside the vault. ForeverMoney's three signers control the token users hold and the pool every batch passes through. Chainlink is mid-migration on the lane with its independent risk check switched off. Bittensor's sudo multisig rewrites precompile behaviour almost weekly. None of them is on a timelock. The practical response is to make the Bittensor adapter the deliberately replaceable part, to turn the ForeverMoney dependency into an explicit relationship with a documented interface and advance notice of changes, and to tell users plainly which outside parties they are trusting.

The second shift is about scale. At beta size, bridge fees are rounding errors and the contracts' own limits rarely bind. What binds is the depth of one market on Base, the gas allowance of one precompile, and the per-message limit of one token pool. Cascade will outgrow Base's TAO market, roughly $55–70k of same-day trading at 2% slippage, long before it outgrows its outflow cap. That makes TVL caps a function of ForeverMoney TAO liquidity on Base and Robinhood Chain, not of contract parameters. The order of work follows from that: talk to ForeverMoney, measure delivery gas on a fork, revise the spec along the fourteen rows above, and only then build the keeper and the app flow around them.
