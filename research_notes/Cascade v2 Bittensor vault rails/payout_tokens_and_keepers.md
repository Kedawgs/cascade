## Key question 1: Which bridged TAO token do users hold on each chain (Base, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon, Arbitrum), and what are its properties?

### Takeaway
On Base the only workable asset is ForeverMoney's CCIP-bridged TAO (`0xf3081494B87e8D5fb7960f066E931D1D0e6E3d67`, 18 decimals, plain non-rebasing mint/burn ERC-20 whose only minter/burner is the Chainlink BurnMintTokenPool and whose admin is a 2-of-3 Safe). It is the same token on Robinhood Chain. No ForeverMoney TAO exists on Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon or Arbitrum; Ethereum has a liquid but custodial, single-operator "wTAO" that cannot be bridged to Bittensor by CCIP, BNB has a dust-liquidity wTAO, and Polygon/Arbitrum have nothing usable.

### Inferences
- The Base token's surface (AccessControl MINTER/BURNER roles held only by the CCIP pool, `getCCIPAdmin`, `maxSupply`) is the Chainlink BurnMintERC20 pattern. It is almost certainly a plain ERC-20: no rebase hooks, no fee-on-transfer, no permit, not upgradeable. The verified source could not be pulled from Basescan without an API key, so "no fee-on-transfer" is inferred from the pattern, not read from source.
- Yield does not accrue to holders. Bridged TAO is a fixed 1:1 claim: the PoR excludes "unclaimed rewards" from backing and the reserve rate sits at ~100.07%, so root-staking rewards on the vault's TAO stay with ForeverMoney's vault rather than inflating holder balances. That is exactly what the front door wants (a plain asset), but it also means users forgo yield while TAO sits in the open batch or in `claimable`.
- Because the Base pool has no allowlist and the router is permissionless, `CascadeFrontDoor`'s adapter can call `ccipSend` with TAO itself; nothing requires ForeverMoney's gateway on the Base side.
- Design risk: what CCIP delivers on Bittensor EVM is ForeverMoney's own WTAO ERC-20 (`0xc5b6…0256`), not native TAO. That token is not a WETH9-style wrapper (`deposit()` and value transfers revert) and holds no native TAO; the backing is root-staked TAO in the AlphaVault on Finney. A Cascade vault that receives WTAO directly would hold a claim it cannot stake until it is redeemed through ForeverMoney's gateway. The alternative is the documented route (CCIP message to ForeverMoney's gateway with "liquid delivery", which lands native TAO at a Finney SS58 address, so it could be sent to the vault's mirror address), but then the batch message and the TAO arrive separately, which conflicts with the spec's `msg.value == amount` / atomic-delivery assumptions. This needs ForeverMoney's gateway ABI or SDK before the vault-side adapter is designed.
- Rate limits cap one CCIP message at 5,000 TAO (~$1.35M at $270.80) per lane direction, and the bucket refills at 0.6 TAO/s (51,840 TAO/day; ~2.3 h to refill from empty). Any `closeBatch` with `totalDeposits > 5,000 TAO` must split or wait; the front door's `depositCap` of 100k TAO-equivalent (open question 4) is far above a single message.
- Decimals: user-chain TAO and WTAO are 18-decimal; native TAO is 9-decimal (rao). The vault must convert 1e18 <-> 1e9 when staking through the precompile, and sub-rao dust (less than 1e9 wei) cannot be staked.
- For Ethereum and BNB Chain, the CCIP lane exists at the router level but ForeverMoney's TAO pool only knows Base and Robinhood; a front door on either chain would need ForeverMoney to deploy a new BurnMint pool there, or a different token. wTAO on Ethereum is liquid ($4.6M) but custodial, closed-source and single-operator, and has no CCIP path to Bittensor, so it is unsuitable as the front-door asset.

## Key question 2: Can the app turn USDC into bridged TAO on Base (and back) at reasonable cost, which routers can do it, and can swap+deposit be one signature?

### Takeaway
Yes for retail sizes: on 2026-10-21 a $500 round trip cost about 0.6% and $5,000 about 0.9% through KyberSwap on Base, but the pool set (one Aerodrome Slipstream TAO/USDC pool of ~$266k and one Uniswap v3 TAO/WETH pool of ~$184k) means 2% one-way slippage arrives around $60–70k and $100k costs ~2.9% to buy. KyberSwap, ParaSwap, LI.FI and Relay all route the token today; 1inch, 0x and Odos could not be verified. One-signature swap+deposit is achievable with EIP-5792 batching in Coinbase Smart Wallet and MetaMask or with a zap contract plus Permit2 (the token has no EIP-2612 permit).

**Single-signature swap + deposit**
- MetaMask supports atomic batches through EIP-5792 (`wallet_getCapabilities`, `wallet_sendCalls`, `wallet_getCallsStatus`); batching can prompt the user to upgrade the account under EIP-7702, after which the batch executes as one atomic transaction. — [MetaMask docs: Batch transactions](https://docs.metamask.io/metamask-connect/evm/guides/send-transactions/batch-transactions.md)

- Round-trip cost (buy then sell, Kyber effective prices): $500 ≈ 0.58% ($2.90); $5,000 ≈ 0.88% ($44); $50,000 ≈ 1.6–3.5% depending on whether the private market makers quote (the 183-TAO sell was worse than the 275-TAO sell, which shows how noisy aggregated depth is at that size). Treat Base TAO as a $5k–$20k-per-trade market today.
- 2% one-way depth on the buy side is about $60–70k (1.82% at $50k, 2.21% at $75k). On the sell side, pure-AMM depth looks like ~$55k at 2% (the 1.64% quote at 183 TAO) with RFQ makers sometimes extending it past $100k.
- The deposit UX can use KyberSwap or ParaSwap directly (no integrator fee) rather than LI.FI (0.25% default integrator fee, and a poor sell route). Redemptions back to USDC should quote both and take the better.
- For one signature: (a) with EIP-5792-capable wallets (Coinbase Smart Wallet, MetaMask with 7702 upgrade) batch `[approve/Permit2, swap, deposit]`; (b) for all other wallets add a thin "zap" entry point that takes USDC plus aggregator calldata, swaps, and deposits the resulting TAO for `msg.sender`, using Uniswap Permit2 for the USDC pull so the user signs once. Because the TAO token has no permit, the zap must receive the TAO itself (it does after the swap), so no TAO approval is needed from the user.
- The spec's `deposit(uint256 amount)` records `msg.sender`; a zap contract must therefore deposit on behalf of the user (an `onBehalfOf` parameter or a `depositFor`) or the zap becomes the recorded depositor. This is a small spec change worth deciding now.

### Gaps
- 1inch, 0x and Odos routing of `0xf308…3d67` on Base (API keys or Cloudflare blocks prevented verification).
- Whether Kyber's private-market-maker fills are available to contract-initiated swaps (RFQ fills typically require signed quotes with short expiry).
- Aerodrome/Velodrome's "Aero" consolidation and whether the Slipstream pool address changes (prior note flagged this for ~2026-10-21; not re-checked).

## Key question 3: Multichain — what should Ethereum, BNB Chain and Polygon users do, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
Only Base (and, secondarily, Robinhood Chain) has ForeverMoney TAO plus a CCIP lane to Bittensor. For Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon and Arbitrum the realistic v2 path is to bridge the user's USDC to Base inside the app (Across ≈ 0.01% + ~$0.004 with ~1 s estimated fill; Relay ≈ $0.07 on $500 with ~2 s) and use the Base front door; Relay can even deliver Base TAO directly from Polygon USDC in one step at ~0.7–0.9% impact.

### Inferences
- A front door on Ethereum or BNB Chain is not buildable today without ForeverMoney adding a BurnMint pool on that chain (the issuer controls `TokenAdminRegistry`/pool configuration), and even then the Ethereum-side CCIP network fee ($0.50 out, $1.50 in) plus Ethereum gas would raise batch costs. The cheaper design is "one front door on Base, every other chain bridges USDC to Base in-app".
- Polygon -> Base USDC via Across or Relay costs well under $1 for retail sizes and settles in seconds; the user then still needs a Base transaction (deposit), so the app must either sponsor Base gas (paymaster via a smart wallet) or have the bridge deliver a little ETH. Relay's single-step Polygon USDC -> Base TAO quote (0.73–0.87% impact) bundles the swap but not the deposit.
- Robinhood Chain is a credible second front-door chain (same token, same CCIP lane, ~$1M liquidity) if its user base matters; it needs no new token.

## Key question 4: What do the batches cost (CCIP fees both ways, Bittensor gas, cost per user), what can run `closeBatch`/`processPayouts` automatically in 2026, and who should pay?

### Takeaway
Live `getFee` quotes on 2026-10-21: a Base -> Bittensor message carrying TAO plus a 100-entry batch (6,560 bytes) costs $2.33 at a 1M destination gas limit and $5.06 at 3M (the Bittensor-side gas at 5 gwei dominates; a token-only transfer is $0.78); the Bittensor -> Base payout message with 100 entries costs only $0.29–$0.34 because Base gas is ~0.006 gwei, plus roughly $1.4–$4 of Bittensor gas for the `processPayouts` transaction itself. Per user that is a few cents at 100 entries and ~$1–2 for a lone depositor. Chainlink Automation and Gelato Web3 Functions are gone (sunset July 2026 and March 2026), Chainlink CRE can trigger the Base side (not Bittensor), and OpenZeppelin Relayer/Monitor or a plain self-hosted bot covers both chains. Comparable products have the operator pay settlement gas while users pay their own request/claim gas.

### Cited Findings

**CCIP fee model**
- Network fee for token transfers between two non-Ethereum chains: $0.25 (or $0.225 in LINK); data-only messaging non-Ethereum -> non-Ethereum: $0.10 ($0.09 in LINK). Executor fee = destination gas fee + optional flat fee; total destination gas = user gas limit + destination gas overhead + verification gas + token-transfer gas + (gas per payload byte × payload size); "unused gas is not refunded"; gas limit is 0 for token-only transfers; fees are added on top of the transfer amount. — [Chainlink CCIP billing](https://docs.chain.link/ccip/billing)
- ForeverMoney charges no bridge fee; its historical example (2026-09-07, 0.1 TAO Bittensor -> Base): CCIP 0.000969 WTAO ≈ $0.25 (= 0.01974 LINK), source gas 0.00362824 TAO ≈ $0.94, total ≈ $1.19; the app adds a 2% safety buffer above the live quote. — [ForeverMoney docs: Fees](https://docs.forevermoney.ai/bridge/fees)

**Live `getFee` quotes, 2026-10-21 (router `getFee(uint64,EVM2AnyMessage)`, receiver = a 20-byte address, `EVMExtraArgsV2{gasLimit, allowOutOfOrderExecution=true}`, fee token = native)**

**How comparable products handle batch cadence and fees**
- Lido: withdrawal requests form a FIFO queue; an Accounting Oracle report finalizes a batch (choosing `_maxShareRate` and batch size from available ether, a timelock and any losses); only `FINALIZE_ROLE` can call `finalize()`; requests are 100 wei–1,000 ETH each; the owner must call `claimWithdrawal()` after finalization (pull); no withdrawal fee is mentioned; rewards accrued while queued are burned to other holders. Normal fulfilment takes 1–5 days; bunker mode caps finalization delay at about 10 days absent slashing. — [Lido docs: WithdrawalQueueERC721](https://docs.lido.fi/contracts/withdrawal-queue-erc721); [Lido help: How long does a withdrawal take](https://help.lido.fi/en/articles/7858315-how-long-does-an-ethereum-withdrawal-take); [Lido docs: Accounting oracle](https://docs.lido.fi/guides/oracle-spec/accounting-oracle/)
- Lagoon (ERC-7540): `requestDeposit` moves assets to a pending silo; the valuation provider proposes a NAV and the curator accepts it by calling `settleDeposit` (all queued deposits settle at one valuation point); users claim shares (pull; from v0.6.0 the curator can push); `requestRedeem`/`settleRedeem` mirror this and redemptions only settle when the curator address holds enough assets; management, performance, entry and exit fees are encoded in the vault; settlement cadence and gas payer are not stated. — [Lagoon docs: Deposit and Redemption flows](https://docs.lagoon.finance/vault/deposit-and-withdraw-flows); [Lagoon docs: Update valuation & settle](https://docs.lagoon.finance/vault/how-to/update-the-vault-valuation-and-settle-requests)
- Veda BoringVault: deposits mint shares immediately in the deposit transaction (optionally `depositWithPermit`), with a vault-specific share lock; withdrawals use `teller.withdraw()` instantly when buffer liquidity suffices, otherwise `queue.requestOnChainWithdraw(asset, shares, discountBps, deadline)`; after a maturity period (typically about 1 hour) third-party solvers fill the request, taking the shares at the user's discount; unfilled requests expire and can be cancelled. — [Veda docs: Deposits & Withdrawals](https://docs.veda.tech/integrations/deposits-and-withdrawals-overview.md)

### Inferences
- Per-batch cost model (2026-10-21 prices, keeper pays): deposit batch = Base `closeBatch` gas (negligible, ~$0.01) + CCIP fee of about $0.78 + Bittensor destination gas at 5 gwei (≈$1.35 per 1M gas) + ~$0.0003 per payload byte. With a gas limit sized at roughly 300k + 30k per entry: 1 entry ≈ $1.25, 10 entries ≈ $1.6 (measured $1.49 at 500k), 100 entries ≈ $5.4 (measured $5.06 at 3M). Payout batch = `processPayouts` gas on Bittensor (unstake + loop; ~$1.4–$4.1 for 1–3M gas) + CCIP fee $0.26–$0.34 ≈ $1.7–$4.4. So a full day at one deposit and one payout batch of 100 entries each costs roughly $7–$10, i.e. $0.07–$0.10 per user; at 10 entries each ≈ $3.3–$6, i.e. $0.33–$0.60 per user; a lone user costs $1–3.
- Over-provisioned gas limits are real money on this lane: 3M vs 1M gas limit adds $2.70 per deposit batch because CCIP does not refund unused destination gas. The keeper should quote `gasLimit` from the entry count rather than a fixed ceiling.
- Automation: (1) Chainlink Automation and Gelato are off the table. (2) CRE can fire a cron on Base and call a consumer contract through the KeystoneForwarder, but the forwarder call carries no ETH, so a small consumer contract must hold ETH and call `closeBatch{value: fee}`; CRE cannot touch Bittensor EVM (not a supported network), its pricing is private, and the private registry allows 3 workflows per org. (3) OpenZeppelin Relayer (self-hosted, any EVM chain by RPC) with Monitor or an external cron covers both chains and keeps keys in Vault/KMS/Turnkey; it needs an ops host. (4) A plain self-hosted bot (one funded key per chain, cron every N minutes calling `closeBatch` when `block.timestamp >= start + batchInterval` or the batch is full, and `processPayouts` on Bittensor) is the least-moving-parts option and the only one that works on Bittensor EVM today. Recommended: self-hosted bot or OZ Relayer for both chains; CRE as an optional second trigger on Base for liveness, since `closeBatch` is permissionless.
- Who pays: every comparable has the operator (oracle, curator, protocol) pay the settlement transaction and users pay only their own request/claim gas; Veda is the exception where users pay an explicit discount to third-party solvers. At $0.07–$0.60 per user per day the keeper-pays model in the spec is cheap and simpler; a pro-rata deduction is only worth its complexity if batches are routinely 1–3 users, and even then a `minDeposit` of a few hundred dollars bounds the worst-case share to under 1%.
- Batch interval: at today's fees a 1-day interval is overkill for cost (batches cost dollars, not tens of dollars); 6-hourly batches would cost roughly $30–$40/day at full fill and still a few dollars/day on quiet days because empty batches are rolled without a bridge send.

### Gaps
- Who pays destination gas for CRE writes (the DON submits via the forwarder; docs do not say whether the workflow owner is billed). CRE pricing is not public.
- Whether OpenZeppelin Relayer has a built-in cron/scheduler (plugins exist; the overview page does not describe scheduling).
- KeeperHub chain list (whether Bittensor EVM is supported) and pricing.
- Actual gas used by `processPayouts`/`onBridgeMessage` for 100 entries (the 300k + 30k/entry rule is an estimate from SSTORE costs, not a measured number); measure on a fork before fixing the gas limit.
- Whether CCIP's `allowOutOfOrderExecution = true` is required on the 1.6 Bittensor lanes (V1 and V2 extraArgs both quoted successfully; execution semantics were not tested).

## Key question 5: Onramp reality check — can a new user go card/Apple Pay -> USDC on Base -> bridged TAO -> deposit in under ~10 minutes, and what fees stack up?

### Takeaway
Plausible but unproven: Coinbase's hosted guest checkout (debit/Apple Pay) was deprecated on 2026-06-30 in favour of the Headless Onramp API (Apple Pay/Google Pay, US-only, up to $2.5k/week, app approval and pre-verified phone/email required); its fee for cards is 2.5% (ACH 0.5%) plus spread, so a $500 card user pays roughly $13–15 (≈3%) end to end, almost all of it onramp fee, with the swap adding ~0.3% and gas pennies. Delivery timing to Base is not documented. Coinbase cannot deliver TAO on Base (it supports TAO only on the native Bittensor network), so "buy TAO" must mean "buy USDC on Base, then swap".

### Cited Findings
- Coinbase Onramp fees: "There is a 2.5% fee for credit card transactions, and 0.5% fee for ACH"; Apple Pay/Google Pay have no separately listed fee (minimum ≈ $5); Onramp includes a price spread; Coinbase pays network fees and charges based on its estimate; USDC can be fee-free for "select eligible partners" through a subsidy program. — [Coinbase CDP Onramp FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq.md)
- "Guest Checkout (debit card, Apple Pay) via the Coinbase-hosted widget is being discontinued", deprecated 2026-06-30; the replacement is the Headless Onramp API, available once the app is approved; guest checkout was available without a Coinbase account in the US, UK and Canada. — [Coinbase CDP Onramp FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq.md)
- Headless Onramp: cards only via Apple Pay or Google Pay, US-only, purchases capped at up to $2.5K weekly; trial mode has limits and full access requires the onboarding form; Coinbase-hosted Onramp has no developer fees. — [Coinbase CDP Onramp overview](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/onramp-overview)
- The headless order endpoint supports `GUEST_CHECKOUT_APPLE_PAY` and `GUEST_CHECKOUT_GOOGLE_PAY`; the example buys USDC on Base with USD; `phoneNumber`, `email`, `phoneNumberVerifiedAt` and `agreementAcceptedAt` are required and enforced server-side; a limits endpoint returns remaining capacity for a phone number previously verified by OTP; webhooks report Apple Pay order success. — [Coinbase CDP: Create an onramp order](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/v2/rest-api/onramp/create-an-onramp-order); [Coinbase CDP: Get onramp user limits](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/v2/rest-api/onramp/get-onramp-user-limits)
- Older guest-checkout guidance: US residents with a debit card could onramp up to $500/week with a $5 minimum; Dynamic's integration (last modified 2026-06-25) shows an Apple Pay -> USDC -> Base sample order. — [Dynamic docs: Coinbase onramp](https://www.dynamic.xyz/docs/money-and-funding/coinbase-onramp)
- Coinbase's TAO asset entry accepts only SS58 addresses (`^5[1-9A-HJ-NP-Za-km-z]{47}$`). — [Coinbase currencies API](https://api.coinbase.com/v2/currencies/crypto)
- Swap cost at $500: 0.31% buy / 0.30% sell (Kyber), gas $0.01 (see Q2). CCIP/keeper share at 100-entry batches: ≈ $0.05–$0.10 per user per cycle (see Q4).

### Inferences
- Fee stack for a $500 card user: onramp 2.5% ($12.50) + spread (not quantified) -> ~$487 USDC on Base; swap 0.31% ($1.50) + gas ($0.01) -> ~1.78 TAO; deposit gas < $0.01; keeper share $0.05–$1.25 depending on batch fill if ever passed through. Total ≈ 2.9% + spread (≈ $14–$15). Exit: swap 0.30% ($1.50) + claim gas; offramping to a bank requires a Coinbase account per the FAQ. ACH users pay ~0.5% + spread instead of 2.5%.
- Time: swap and deposit are seconds on Base; the user's shares arrive only after the batch closes (interval TBD) plus CCIP delivery, so "deposit in 10 minutes" is true for the user's actions, not for share issuance. Apple Pay delivery time to Base and the first-time phone/email verification time are not documented, so the 10-minute claim cannot be verified from sources.
- An app that is not yet an approved CDP partner cannot offer Apple Pay guest checkout at all; the alternative is a Coinbase-account flow (full KYC, global) or a third-party onramp. Zero-fee USDC is partner-gated.
- Because Coinbase can only send native TAO to an SS58 address, a direct "buy native TAO into the vault" path would bypass Base entirely but would deliver to the vault's mirror address with no way to attribute the deposit to a user; it is not usable for the front door without a per-user deposit address scheme.

### Gaps
- Coinbase Onramp payment-to-delivery latency for USDC on Base and the time to complete first-time phone/email verification (no source found).
- Apple Pay/debit fee percentage under the Headless API (the FAQ lists only credit card 2.5% and ACH 0.5%).
- Whether any other onramp (MoonPay, Stripe, Transak) delivers USDC on Base with lower card fees; not researched in this track.
